Complex plasmas consist of microscopic particles in a plasma environment where the
charged particles form a strongly-coupled many-particle system. Complex plasmas
provide a wide and interdisciplinary research field that enables new insights into
fundamental physical properties of matter on the kinetic level of individual particles,
including e.g.:
• Statistical physics, nonlinear dynamics and phase transitions
• Active matter
• Planetary physics and dust transport and mitigation
• Particles as diagnostics in plasmas

Th complex plasma research in COMPACT will advance applications in industrial plasma
processing, e.g. etching, plasma coating, safety of fusion devices, or planetary
exploration.

Interesting problems to be tackled in COMPACT are:

## Statistical physics

* Equation of State (EOS): Determine the relation between thermodynamic quantities, such as
temperature T, volume V or pressure p, describing the state of complex plasma matter; thermodynamics 
of systems in which Newton’s third law is “broken” 

* Deviations from an ideal gas, e.g. influence of non-negligible occupied volume of the dust particles

* Transport properties as a response to perturbations, e.g. heat conductivity, viscosity and diffusion

* Non-equilibrium thermodynamics: coupling of the thermodynamic properties of the dust particles to the 
non-equilibrium environment of the plasma particles (electrons and ions)

* Fluctuation theorem, e.g. explore the probability of small dust systems to loose energy 
relative to the probability to gain energy

## Phase transitions 

* Solid-liquid phase transitions: fully resolved atomistic dynamics with complex plasmas
to study the elementary processes governing crystallization; homogeneous and heterogeneous crystallization 
processes

* Supercooled liquids: kinetics of slow relaxation processes 

* Glass transitions: quenching of liquids below their melting point by cooling or compression in a
manner that prevents crystallization

## Nonlinear dynamics

* Turbulence: study of active turbulence through internal instabilities; determination of energy cascades

* Nonlinear waves: drive large-amplitude waves; influence of agglomeration, size distributions and strong-coupling effects; 
shock waves; rogue waves

## Active and non-spherical particles

* Structure and order in systems of non-spherical particles: phase diagram of ellipsoidal or dumbbell-shaped particles; 
emergence of orientational order and liquid crystal phases

* Statistical physics of diatomic colloidal matter: EOS of ellipsoidal or dumbbell-shaped particles; 
influence of rotational degree of freedom

* Mode dynamcis in systems of non-spherical particles: visco-elastic properties; rheology of non-spherical particle systems

* Active particle systems: statistical properties of active particles; structure of dust systems with active particles

* Rheology and collective behavior of active particle systems; rheology, turbulence and sound waves in dust systems 
with active particles; giant number fluctuations; emergence of cooperative/collective behavior

## Planetary physics

* Aerosol coagulation: validation of coagulation rate constant models

* Contact charging events upon collision: particle drifts in non-plasma situations

## Particles as diagnostics in plasmas

* Particles as floating probes: charging and potentials; UV charging; afterglow plasmas 

* Particles as force sensors; wave dispersions; electric field forces

* Electron depletion effects: correlation with the electric characteristics of the plasma
